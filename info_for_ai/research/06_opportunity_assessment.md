# Research-opportunity assessment

## Activation

Use this module to prioritize research directions with a reasonable chance of producing publishable results, with explicit weight given to industrial application.

## Method

1. Define the field, available resources, time horizon, team capabilities, access to data or hardware, and desired forms of impact. Use and label scenarios when these are unknown.
2. Map the state of the art, benchmarks, industrial needs, patents, standards, and adoption barriers. Separate demonstrated demand from an imaginable application.
3. For each direction, assess novelty, tractability, evidence of need, differentiation from the state of the art, evaluation availability, cost, third-party dependence, technical risk, reproducibility, and route to publication or transfer.
4. Prefer falsifiable hypotheses and bounded experiments that generate evidence even when the central hypothesis fails. Never claim that a direction guarantees publication or return.

## Output

For each opportunity provide:

- **Proposal and problem:** what would be researched and who benefits.
- **Evidence and maturity:** relevant state of the art, need signals, and gaps.
- **Scientific and industrial potential:** impact mechanism, users or sectors, and adoption obstacles.
- **Minimum viable validation:** hypothesis, data, method, benchmark, metric, and success or rejection criterion.
- **Risks and mitigations:** technical, data, competitive, legal, and reproducibility risks with concrete alternatives.

Finish with a reasoned high, medium, or low priority matrix rather than fictitious numerical precision. Explain which assumptions would change the ranking.
