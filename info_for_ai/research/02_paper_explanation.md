# Paper explanation

## Activation

Use this module when given a DOI, link, or PDF to read, preserve traceable notes about, explain, or answer questions about in study or technical mode.

## Prepare the paper

1. Resolve the supplied identifier or inspect the PDF and identify its title, authors, date, version, venue, DOI, and license or accessibility.
2. Read all relevant sections, figures, tables, references, and appendices. State which parts are unavailable or illegible.
3. Save working notes in a Markdown file when continued analysis requires them. Include the full reference, question, contributions, definitions and notation, method, results, assumptions, limitations, relationship to prior work, open questions, and section or page locations for important equations, figures, and claims.
4. Label paper content, your deductions, and external context separately. Notes support later discussion but do not replace the original paper.

## Study mode

Use study mode when asked to understand, review, derive step by step, or connect ideas. Assume a strong foundation in mathematics and physics, familiarity with tensor networks and quantum computing, and basic computer science knowledge.

- Explain each concept before relying on it while preserving its physical or mathematical assumptions.
- Use analogies only when accurate and state their limits.
- Add examples and links to physics, mathematics, and basic computer science when useful, without substituting analogy for proof.
- Develop calculations and proofs deeply enough to check. Ask for the desired level only when the request remains genuinely ambiguous.

## Technical mode

Use technical mode for specialist rigor, methodological comparison, or connections to other work. Prioritize notation, assumptions, complexity, validity conditions, and empirical evidence. Refer to verified related literature without reteaching fundamentals unless requested.

## Follow-up questions

Identify whether a question concerns the paper's claim, an inference, or a critique. Point to the section, figure, equation, or page when possible. If the paper does not support the answer, say so and separate external context.
